The proforma's Value Growth Trajectory chart and 3-Year Financial Summary have critical math and logic bugs. The individual care setting calculators produce correct values — the problem is how those values get projected over time in the proforma. The three value categories (Doc Quality, Capacity & Efficiency, Retention) should each have fundamentally different onset behaviors, but right now they all scale similarly and the provider rollout is wrong.
BUG 1 (Critical): Provider Count Uses Linear Interpolation Instead of Respecting the Rollout Plan
The user configures a stepped rollout plan:

Y1: 50 providers
Y2: 230 providers
Y3: 500 providers
But the chart and financial summary ignore these steps entirely. Instead, the model linearly interpolates from 50 → 500 across 12 quarters:
| Quarter | Expected Providers | Actual Providers Shown |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 '26 | 50 | 80 |
| Q2 '26 | 50 | 125 |
| Q3 '26 | 50 | 170 |
| Q4 '26 | 50 | 215 |
| Q1 '27 | 230 | 275 |
By end of Y1, the model shows 215 providers instead of the configured 50. The "Active Providers" row in the Financial Summary confirms this: it shows 215 for 2026, not 50.
This inflates Y1 value by ~4× and makes the proforma non-defensible. A CFO will look at the rollout plan (50 providers in Y1) and the financial summary ($1.5M in Y1) and the math won't reconcile.
The fix: Provider counts must follow the configured rollout steps. Within each year, providers should ramp linearly from the previous year's end count to the current year's target:
Y1 quarters: Hold at 50 (or ramp from 0 → 50 in Q1 if modeling go-live ramp, then hold at 50 for Q2-Q4)
Y2 quarters: Ramp linearly from 50 → 230 across Q1-Q4 of Y2
Y3 quarters: Ramp linearly from 230 → 500 across Q1-Q4 of Y3
This means:
Q1 '26: ~50, Q2 '26: 50, Q3 '26: 50, Q4 '26: 50
Q1 '27: ~95, Q2 '27: ~140, Q3 '27: ~185, Q4 '27: ~230
Q1 '28: ~298, Q2 '28: ~365, Q3 '28: ~433, Q4 '28: 500
The "Active Providers" row in the Financial Summary should reflect the average active providers for each year (Y1: 50, Y2: ~140, Y3: ~365), not the linearly interpolated values currently shown (215, 478, 500).
This single fix will cascade through all three value categories and correct the Financial Summary totals.


BUG 2 (Critical): The Three Value Categories Must Have Visually Distinct Onset Curves
Right now all three value categories (Doc Quality, Capacity & Efficiency, Retention) scale with roughly the same slope — they all just follow the (incorrect) provider ramp. The whole point of the three-category framework is that they have fundamentally different time-to-value behaviors. Here's exactly how each should work:
A. Doc Quality (IMMEDIATE — accrues from Day 1)
Doc Quality includes wRVU improvement, HCC capture, denial prevention, and DRG accuracy. These values are created the moment a provider starts using Abridge — the AI produces better notes from day one.
How to calculate per quarter:
quarterly_doc_quality = per_provider_annual_doc_value × active_providers_in_quarter / 4
No delay. No phasing. No ramp-up factor. If 50 providers go live on Month 1 and each generates $9,214/year in doc quality value ($460,688 ÷ 50), then Q1 value is:
$9,214 × 50 / 4 = $115,171
(Adjust for partial months if go-live is mid-quarter.)
On the chart: Doc Quality should be the FIRST band to appear and should step up immediately whenever new provider cohorts go live. It has the most linear/stepped shape of the three bands.
B. Capacity & Efficiency (90-DAY DELAY — then accrues at full rate)
Capacity & Efficiency includes patient access, throughput, locum reduction, and similar operational gains. These require the organization to operationalize the freed-up time — you can't fill appointment slots or reduce locum contracts on day one.
How to calculate per quarter:
if (months_since_cohort_golive < 3):
    quarterly_capacity = 0  // within 90-day onset window
else:
    quarterly_capacity = per_provider_annual_capacity_value × active_providers_in_quarter / 4
Current behavior is wrong: Capacity shows $0 in Q1, $0 in Q2, $44K in Q3. With a Month 1 go-live and 90-day delay, capacity should first appear in Q2 (Month 4 is early Q2), not Q3. The current behavior looks like a ~6-month delay.
On the chart: The Capacity band should start $0 for approximately one quarter after go-live, then appear and track closely (but lagging) behind the Doc Quality band. There should be a visible 1-quarter gap between when the Doc Quality band appears and when the Capacity band appears. After that initial gap, the slope should be similar to Doc Quality.
C. Retention (PHASED — fundamentally different slope, the slowest to materialize)
Retention includes clinician/nurse retention and wellbeing value. This is the category where time-to-value is genuinely different — you can't prevent turnover in Month 1. Organizations need time to see burnout reduction translate into actual retention improvements.
How to calculate per quarter:
year = current_year_of_deployment  // 1, 2, or 3
phasing = retention_phasing[year]  // e.g., 20%, 65%, 100% from sliders
quarterly_retention = per_provider_annual_retention_value × active_providers_in_quarter × phasing / 4
Current behavior is wrong in multiple ways:

Retention shows $0 for Q1-Q3 of Y1, then suddenly $10K in Q4. It should show small but non-zero values from Q1 onward (at 20% phasing).
At full scale (Q3 '28, 500 providers, 100% phasing), retention shows $267K/quarter but should be $360K/quarter ($144K × 10 × 100% ÷ 4).
The retention band's slope looks proportional to the other two bands — it doesn't have a distinctly different shape.
On the chart — this is the most important visual distinction: The retention band MUST have a visibly concave (accelerating) shape compared to the other two bands:


Y1: Nearly invisible thin sliver. Even at 20% phasing with 50 providers, the quarterly value is only ~$7K. This should be barely visible — and that's the point. It communicates "retention value takes time."
Y2: Noticeable thickening. The 65% phasing kicks in AND more providers are active. The band should visibly step up in thickness at the Y1→Y2 boundary.
Y3: Thick, substantial band. 100% phasing with 500 providers. The band should be a significant portion of the total stack.
The acceleration from Y1 → Y2 → Y3 should be visually obvious. When someone looks at this chart, the retention band should immediately communicate "this value builds over time" without needing to read any labels.
