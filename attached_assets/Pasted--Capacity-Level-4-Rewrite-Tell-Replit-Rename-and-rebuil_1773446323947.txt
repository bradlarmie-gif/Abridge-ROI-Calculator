# Capacity Level 4 — Rewrite

## Tell Replit:

Rename and rebuild Capacity Level 4. It's currently called "Workforce Impact" with FTE-avoidance inputs. That belongs in the Workforce domain, not Capacity. Capacity should stay focused on patient access from start to finish.

## The Full Capacity Progression (for context)

```
Level 1: Time Recovering
Providers are faster. Time savings exist but aren't aggregated or deployed.

Level 2: Quantified
Aggregate recovered hours are calculated and reviewed by leadership.

Level 3: Deployed
Recovered time is allocated to patient access. Additional patients are being seen.

Level 4: Access Impact Measured
The downstream impact of additional access is being tracked — wait times,
referral patterns, care capture, and growth planning.
```

## Level 4 New Name: "Access Impact Measured"

"Your organization isn't just seeing more patients — it's measuring what that access means. Wait time improvements, referral capture, downstream utilization, and growth planning are informed by recovered capacity data."

## Why This Works

**Level 3 → Level 4 distinction is clear:**
- Level 3: "We see more patients." (volume)
- Level 4: "We see more patients AND we're measuring what that access created downstream." (impact)

**The checkboxes are things a CMO would know:**
- Did our wait times improve? Yes/no — they track third-next-available.
- Are we capturing more referrals? Yes/no — referral leakage is a known metric.
- Are we measuring downstream revenue from new visits? Yes/no — some track this, most don't.
- Are we using this data for growth planning? Yes/no — the most strategic organizations do.

**The downstream revenue input is the financial unlock:**
A new patient visit at $200 is the Level 3 story. But that patient also gets labs, imaging, follow-ups, procedures, referrals to specialists. The downstream revenue per new patient can be $150-$500+ depending on specialty. Level 4 captures that multiplier effect.

This is genuinely aspirational — most organizations don't track downstream revenue from recovered capacity. But the ones who do have a dramatically bigger capacity story. That's what makes it Level 4.

**The FTE-avoidance content that was previously at Level 4:**
Move the checkboxes about avoided hires, redeployed providers, and FTEs deferred to the Workforce domain where they belong. If Workforce doesn't have a natural home for them, they could fit at Workforce Level 4 as part of the "labor spend structurally reduced" story — organizations that avoided hiring because of recovered capacity are seeing it in their labor line.

Or simply remove FTE avoidance from the tool entirely. It's a strong concept but it straddles Capacity and Workforce awkwardly. If it doesn't have a clean home, it's better to cut it than to confuse the domain boundaries.
