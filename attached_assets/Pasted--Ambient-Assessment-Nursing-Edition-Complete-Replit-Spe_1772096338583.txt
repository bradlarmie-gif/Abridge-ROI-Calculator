# Ambient Assessment: Nursing Edition — Complete Replit Spec

## What This Is

A strategic alignment tool for nursing leadership. Before anyone talks about ROI or time savings, this helps a CNO or CNIO get clear on what their nursing program is trying to accomplish and where reducing documentation burden fits into that picture.

The output is clarity: "For our organization, the case for investing in nursing documentation technology is about workload relief and retention — and here's how we'd frame that conversation internally."

Same UX, same brand, same card treatments, same typography as the outpatient Ambient Assessment. Different structure because the problem is different.

## SCREEN 5: YOUR RECOMMENDED FOCUS

**Header:**
HOW TO THINK ABOUT THE INVESTMENT CASE
Based on your priorities, here's how we'd think about framing the conversation for your organization.

**Main card:**

```
YOUR SITUATION

Your nursing program is focused on retention, staffing
costs, and nurse wellbeing. Documentation burden
connects to all three — but not in the same way or
on the same timeline.

HOW TO FRAME THE CONVERSATION

Lead with nurse wellbeing.
Your survey data shows documentation is a top concern
for your nurses. When you can show that a specific
intervention directly addresses the thing nurses are
telling you is a problem, that's a compelling starting
point. It's immediate, it's tangible, and nurses will
feel the difference.

Anchor the financial case in overtime.
End-of-shift documentation is contributing an estimated
1,733 hours of OT annually at your organization. This
is the most measurable near-term financial metric
because the connection between documentation time
and end-of-shift OT is direct.

Build the longer-term case around retention.
Your turnover rate represents $2.8M in annual cost.
Documentation burden is one of the factors your
organization has identified. The retention impact
builds over time — as workload improves, satisfaction
improves, and the decision to stay becomes easier for
your nurses. This is a 6-12 month story, not a
30-day story.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR EVALUATING TECHNOLOGY

Any investment in documentation technology for nursing
should be evaluated against these priorities. The
questions to ask:

• Does it meaningfully reduce documentation time
  during and after the shift?
• Can we measure the impact on overtime within
  60-90 days?
• Will nurses actually feel the difference in their
  daily workflow?
• Does it position us to address retention over
  the longer term?

If the answer to these questions is yes, the
investment case aligns with what your nursing
program is trying to accomplish.
```

**This section doesn't mention Abridge.** It mentions "documentation technology" and "any investment in this space." The CNO reads evaluation criteria that Abridge happens to meet — but the tool never says that. The tool gives them a framework for evaluating. The conversation about Abridge specifically happens naturally after.

**If they only selected priorities with weak documentation connections (e.g., only "Future Readiness"):**

```
YOUR SITUATION

Your nursing program is focused on future readiness —
building the infrastructure for clinical AI, value-based
care, and data-driven practice.

Documentation burden reduction supports this goal by
creating the structured, complete documentation
foundation these initiatives require. However, the
financial case for documentation technology is
typically stronger when paired with a more immediate
priority like retention, staffing costs, or wellbeing.

HOW TO THINK ABOUT IT

If documentation technology investment needs a
near-term financial justification, consider whether
retention or staffing cost pressures — even if they
aren't your top strategic priority today — could
provide the financial anchor for a business case
that also advances your future readiness goals.
```

Honest. Helpful. Not pushy.

## GLOBAL UX RULES (same as outpatient)

1. No input has a pre-filled default value on number fields
2. Checkboxes are never pre-checked
3. Radio buttons are never pre-selected
4. Empty inputs show "—" in the sidebar, never "0"
5. All benchmark text: "National average: X (Source)" — never "Abridge reports"
6. All estimates labeled: "Estimates based on your inputs. Individual results vary."
7. All inputs persist when navigating between screens
8. Same card treatments, typography, colors, and spacing as outpatient
