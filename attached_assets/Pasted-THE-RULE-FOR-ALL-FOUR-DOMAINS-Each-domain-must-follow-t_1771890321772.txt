THE RULE FOR ALL FOUR DOMAINS

Each domain must follow this maturity arc:

Level 1 → Benefit exists, not structurally captured
Level 2 → Benefit locally applied, not systemized
Level 3 → Benefit intentionally managed
Level 4 → Benefit institutionalized and governed

And each level must:

Reflect real outpatient behavior

Justify a progressively deeper input

Trigger different math behavior

Be defensible in a CFO conversation

No fluff.
No multipliers.
No vibes.

DOMAIN 1 — CAPACITY

(Only rewriting the 4 cards)

Current header:
“Where does recovered time actually go?”

Keep that.

Now replace the four cards with this:

LEVEL 1

Time Saved, Not Deployed

Recovered documentation time reduces friction for providers — but no structural change in access, templates, or panel growth.

Math behavior:
Redeployment = 0%

Input behavior:
No additional inputs required.

This level says:
“You are experiencing efficiency, not capacity.”

That hits.

LEVEL 2

Ad Hoc Access Relief

Recovered time occasionally reduces backlog or enables extra visits, but without formal scheduling redesign.

New Input Triggered:
Estimated % of recovered time used for additional visits (0–25%)

Math behavior:
Recovered hours × estimated redeployment %

Now the user must declare whether time is actually becoming visits.

That creates tension.

LEVEL 3

Structured Access Expansion

Visit templates or provider schedules have been intentionally modified to increase throughput using recovered time.

New Inputs:
Additional patients per month
Revenue per visit

Math behavior:
Patients × revenue × 12

Now we override estimation with declared structural change.

That feels real.

LEVEL 4

Institutionalized Capacity Strategy

Recovered time is embedded into panel planning, provider FTE modeling, and executive-level access targets.

New Inputs:
Net visit growth per provider
OR FTE-equivalent expansion

Math behavior:
Provider-level expansion × revenue × 12

This is enterprise-grade capacity governance.

That is Level 4.

Notice what we did:

We did NOT invent overtime.
We did NOT invent multipliers.
We did NOT invent fuzzy percentages.

We defined operational states.

Why This Feels Better

Because when someone selects a level, they must ask:

“Are we structurally redesigning access?”
or
“Are we just saving time?”

That’s category framing.

That’s how you make competitors look shallow.
