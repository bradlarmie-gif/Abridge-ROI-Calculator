THE APPROACH
Here's what I think you need to do:
1. Kill the report-card energy
The current PDF feels like it's grading them. Score: 59%. Dimensions. Benchmarks. Gap.
Instead, make it feel like a clinical assessment—like a doctor reviewing a patient's chart. Not judgmental. Just clear-eyed and thorough. A doctor doesn't say "your cholesterol is bad." They say "your cholesterol is 240. The target range is 150-200. Here's what that means for you specifically."
2. Make page 2 about THEM, not about scores
The first real page should feel personal. Right now it jumps straight to "YOUR VALUE REALIZATION SCORE: 59%." That's cold.
What if it started with:
CopyYOUR ORGANIZATION

10 providers · 25,000 annual encounters
Currently using ambient AI documentation

You've taken the most important step—you've invested in ambient AI 
for your providers. This assessment looks at how that investment is 
performing across four dimensions, and where the opportunity lies.
Then the score. Then the breakdown. The score hits differently when it's contextualized by who they are first.
3. Make the benchmark page feel like education, not comparison
Right now page 3 says "66% of benchmark" in red next to each metric. That reads as a grade.
What if instead, each dimension told a micro-story:
CopyADOPTION: 50%
Abridge Benchmark: 76%

Half of your providers are using AI documentation consistently. 
When adoption is below 60%, it typically reflects workflow friction 
rather than a technology problem—the tool isn't fitting naturally 
into how providers work.
Same data. But now it's explaining, not grading.
4. Make the opportunity page feel like possibility, not failure
"The Gap" → "The Opportunity Ahead"
And instead of just showing $36K, show what that means in human terms:
Copy$36K in potential additional value per year

What does that look like?

- 475 additional hours of provider time reclaimed
- 6,500 more encounters with AI documentation support  
- Meaningful improvement in provider experience
Numbers become real when they become tangible.
5. Make the math page feel like transparency, not a spreadsheet
The math page should feel like you're sitting across from someone and saying "let me show you how we got here." Not like an accountant handing over a tax return.
The NaN bug aside—the structure is actually close. But the framing copy matters:
Copy"Every number traces back to your inputs. We've shown our assumptions 
so you can adjust anything that doesn't match your reality."
6. Make the patterns page feel like wisdom, not a sales pitch
This is where you teach. Right now it reads well but the "bottom line" tips into sales territory.
The soul of this page should be: "We've been in this with a lot of organizations. Here's what we've noticed matters most."
7. Make the summary page feel like a beginning, not an ending
Right now it feels like a final exam recap. It should feel like the opening slide of a conversation:
Copy"This is your starting point. The numbers tell a clear story—there's 
meaningful opportunity ahead. The next step is understanding which 
specific factors matter most for your organization."
