This is a really important conversation. Let me share my unfiltered thoughts on EXPAND and what makes it fundamentally different from SWITCH.

The Soul Difference
SWITCH = "Here's what you're leaving on the table with your current vendor" → Educational, revealing gaps
EXPAND = "Here's the value you're creating — and here's how to prove it to your CFO" → Defensive, ammunition for internal stakeholders

The SWITCH user is being educated. The EXPAND user is being armed.

The Hard Truth About What We're Building
The challenge isn't showing pretty numbers. The challenge is that a skeptical CFO will try to destroy every number in a quarterly business review.

So the question becomes: How do we build something that survives that scrutiny?

The Architecture Problem
SWITCH has 4 sliders. Clean.

EXPAND needs potentially 7+ metrics × before/after = 14+ inputs minimum.

That's overwhelming if we show it all at once. So we need:

Metric selection first — "Which of these are you tracking?"
Progressive data entry — Only show cards for metrics they selected
Tiered value output — Not everything needs to be dollars
My Proposed Mental Model: Three Tiers
Tier 1: Core Financial Value (CFO-defensible dollars)
wRVU per encounter improvement
Level of Service improvement
These are the numbers that directly hit revenue. They WILL be scrutinized.
Tier 2: Operational Efficiency (dollars with caveats)
Time in notes reduction
Work after work time reduction
User chooses: "Do you want to convert this to dollars?" with methodology options:
No conversion (just show the time)
Patient access conversion (more visits = revenue)
Overtime reduction (direct cost savings)
Provider retention value (strategic)
Tier 3: Quality Indicators (proof points, not dollars)
Chart closure rate
Diagnosis capture
