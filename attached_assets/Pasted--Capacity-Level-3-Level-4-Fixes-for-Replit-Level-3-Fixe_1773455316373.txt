# Capacity Level 3 + Level 4 — Fixes for Replit

---

## Level 3 Fixes

### Remove the auto-populated patient range before user enters data
Currently shows "1,800-4,800 additional patients annually" as a headline before the user types anything. Remove this calculated range. The input field should be empty. The estimated impact card should show: "Enter additional patients per provider per month to calculate access revenue."

Keep the benchmark text below the input: "Structured access redesign: 3-8 patients/provider/month. Annualized over 11 clinical months (230 working days)."

### Make data confidence toggle visually change the output

When **Measured** is selected, the estimated impact headline shows:
```
$880K in access revenue annually
Based on your confirmed scheduling data.
```

When **Estimated** is selected:
```
$880K in access revenue annually (estimated)
Based on your organization's estimate. Validate
with scheduling data to confirm.
```

When **Aspirational** is selected:
```
$880K access revenue target
This is a planning target, not confirmed data.
```

The framing text below the number should change with each selection. Right now Measured and Estimated produce the same output which defeats the purpose.

### Add visit type note below the formula
Below the access revenue formula, add: "Revenue per visit uses your baseline input of $[X]. If additional visits are typically shorter or lower-complexity than your average, adjust revenue per visit in your baseline settings."

## Level 4 — Rebuild

### Remove the downstream revenue per visit input entirely

Drop the "Downstream revenue per additional visit" field and the $150-$500 range. Here's why:

1. The $150-$500 range dramatically understates what a new patient is actually worth to a system downstream. Real downstream value per new patient is $1,500-$5,000+ in the first year depending on specialty and complexity.

2. But putting ANY downstream revenue number in a formula creates an attribution problem. The downstream utilization depends on clinical needs, referral patterns, insurance, and dozens of factors that have nothing to do with ambient documentation. A CFO will challenge the connection immediately.

3. The downstream story is better as a conversation point, not a calculation. When you're in the room you can say: "And by the way, every one of those 4,400 new visits is a new patient entering your system who generates downstream labs, imaging, referrals, and follow-ups. You can estimate what that's worth." That's more powerful as a verbal insight than as a formula that tries to be precise about something imprecise.

### Keep Level 4 focused on access outcomes being tracked

Level 4's value is in the checkboxes — showing which access outcomes the organization is measuring as a result of recovered capacity. That's the strategic maturity. Most organizations will check 2-3 of 6, which reveals the gap.

### Why this is better:

The last paragraph — "Every additional patient entering your system also generates downstream activity... The full system value is significantly higher" — plants the idea without trying to calculate it. The executive reads that and thinks "yeah, each of those patients is probably worth $3,000-$5,000 to us over the year." You can have that conversation verbally. But the tool doesn't try to put a formula around something that's hard to attribute and easy to challenge.

Level 4's value is the checkboxes. An organization that checks 4 of 6 is strategically managing access. An organization that checks 1 is just counting visits. That gap IS the Level 3 → Level 4 distinction. The downstream revenue was adding complexity without adding credibility.
