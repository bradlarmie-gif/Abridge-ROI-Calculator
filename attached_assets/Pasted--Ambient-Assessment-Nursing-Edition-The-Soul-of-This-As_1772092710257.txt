# Ambient Assessment: Nursing Edition

## The Soul of This Assessment

A CNO or VP of Nursing is thinking about five things every day: can I keep my nurses, can I afford my staffing model, are my nurses burned out, are my patients getting enough attention, and is my documentation defensible. Those are the strategic questions.

This assessment doesn't ask them to estimate minutes per flowsheet. It asks them to reflect on where their nursing program is under pressure — and then helps them see where reducing documentation burden could matter most. It's a strategic conversation about their nursing program that happens to surface where ambient documentation fits.

The outpatient version asks: "How mature is your ambient ROI strategy?"
The nursing version asks: **"Where is your nursing program under the most pressure — and where would reduced documentation burden make the biggest difference?"**

## Important Constraint

Abridge for nursing is focused on **flowsheet documentation** specifically. That's the product reality. The assessment should be honest about this — it's about documentation burden broadly, but the intervention we're discussing is flowsheet time savings. The pathways we explore are the ones that connect to that specific time savings.

---

## The Structure

Same feel as outpatient: baseline inputs → strategic domains → summary. But instead of maturity levels (which don't apply because they don't have the product yet), each domain asks the CNO to assess where they are today and whether that area is under pressure. The assessment reveals their pressure points and connects them to how documentation burden reduction could help.

**Four domains, mirroring the strategic concerns of nursing leadership:**

1. **Workforce Stability** — Are you keeping your nurses?
2. **Labor Cost** — Can you afford your staffing model?
3. **Nurse Experience** — Are your nurses spending time where it matters?
4. **Care Quality and Compliance** — Is your documentation supporting quality and safety?

## Why This Version Has Soul

1. **It meets the CNO where they are.** Workforce stability, labor cost, nurse experience, care quality — these are the four things keeping nursing leadership up at night. The assessment is organized around THEIR concerns, not around a product's features.

2. **The level selection is strategic self-reflection, not a quiz.** "Is turnover manageable, elevated, a strategic threat, or actively managed?" — a CNO can answer that in 2 seconds and it immediately frames the conversation.

3. **It doesn't pretend to know their numbers.** If they don't enter turnover rate, it doesn't guess. If OT isn't their issue, it says so. The honesty is the credibility.

4. **It connects to flowsheets without being about flowsheets.** The assessment is about nursing program strategy. The connection to ambient flowsheet documentation is natural and organic — it surfaces where documentation burden is creating pressure, and then shows where reducing it would help. The product fits the problem, not the other way around.

5. **It flows naturally into the ROI calculator.** The assessment says "your priority pathways are retention and OT." The ROI calculator models those pathways. The CNO walks into the ROI conversation already knowing what matters. That's a 10x better conversation than starting cold.
