# The Attain Align Bar

Attain is a **value-attainment strategy** we build *with* a partner, after Explore
has already sold the promise. The Align chapter is not a calculator and not a
sizing exercise. Its job is one thing:

> **When the partner finishes answering an Align walk, we are in lock step on their
> north star and on what success looks like to them.** The Plan then solidifies it
> into metrics we track, and Progress keeps it moving. The dollar is a byproduct.

Patient Access is the reference implementation. Every value driver, in every care
setting, is held to the same bar.

## The test for every question

A beat earns its place **only if it is something you would genuinely want to know
to get in lock step** with this partner about this goal. If a beat exists only to
feed the math, it fails — move it out of Align (to the Starting Point, to a seeded
assumption, or to the Plan as a tracked metric).

Say it out loud: *"You want to improve your LWBS rate? Before we do anything, let's
get clear on a few things."* Every beat should belong in that sentence.

## The rules

1. **≤ 7 questions per walk** (per mechanism, for multi-mechanism drivers). If it's
   longer, it's a form, not a conversation. Enforced by test.
2. **Name the north star** — the outcome(s) they're actually chasing.
3. **Ground it in their reality** — where it's happening (specialty / zone / population / unit).
4. **The honest gate is mandatory** — every walk must ask, in its own words, *"is
   this actually documentation-driven, or is it something we don't move?"* This is
   what makes the lock step real instead of a pitch. Examples: Access "is the
   bottleneck documentation?"; Revenue "why is it slipping?"; Nursing "how much
   could earlier attention actually change?"; Risk "is the gap at the visit, or
   downstream in coding and submission?"
5. **End on success + stakes** — *what would tell you it's working* (proof signals)
   and *what hitting it opens up* (the unlock).
6. **The number is a byproduct.** It assembles quietly from the Starting Point
   economics + a light realization stance. No calculator beats in the conversation
   (no "dollars per X", no "$ per condition", no volume-to-be-multiplied).
7. **The Plan carries the proof.** For every outcome an Align walk names, the Plan
   must hold the metric that proves it (today → target), and Progress tracks it. A
   walk isn't done until its proof metrics exist in the Plan.

## Dollar vs. tracked (attribution decides)

The number recedes wherever we can't honestly credit it to the documentation:

- **Quiet dollar** where the note carries the billable thing and the number is modest
  and defensible: outpatient & ED E/M level, inpatient DRG weight, patient-access
  throughput, preventable denials (the note establishes necessity), CDI query cost,
  observation-status defense (the note carries the severity).
- **Tracked, not counted** where the value is too far downstream for us to honestly
  own the dollar: **risk / HCC recapture** (a coder has to act on it and it must
  survive RADV extrapolation — a $4M figure built from our own seeds fails the
  smell test), plus **provider retention** and **nursing quality**. These are proven
  by their metric moving (recapture rate 50% → 75%, captured conditions), tracked in
  the Plan, never a dollar we assembled from seeds.

## Reference: the Patient Access shape

`goal → where → honest gate → scope → stance → proof → unlock` — seven beats, each
one a thing you'd want to know, the dollar assembling underneath without ever being
asked for directly. Match the *spirit*, not the literal slots: each driver picks the
≤7 beats that get us in lock step on *its* north star.
